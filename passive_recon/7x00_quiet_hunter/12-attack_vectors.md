# Attack Vectors — Helix Maritime Insurance

## Vector 1: IT Leadership Spear-Phishing via Migration Pretext
The attacker's entry point is the corporate email account of the Infrastructure & Security Lead, Joris Bakker. This attack chain unfolds by leveraging the ongoing corporate migrations to draft a highly credible spear-phishing email. Using the OSINT prerequisites—specifically the CISO's identity and email pattern verified in `9-employee_notes.md` Finding 1, the email migration to ZephyrMail Business confirmed in `10-stack_notes.md` Finding 2, and the CRM migration context from `7-jobs_notes.md` Finding 1—the attacker spoofs an urgent IT alert. The email requests Joris to re-authenticate his credentials on a fake ZephyrMail portal due to the rollout. The probable business impact is devastating: compromising this specific administrative entry point grants the attacker internal routing access, control over cloud identity providers, and the ability to seamlessly exfiltrate sensitive corporate data.

## Vector 2: Supply Chain Compromise via Developer Account
The entry point for this vector is the public GitHub account belonging to backend developer Daan de Vries (`dvries`). The attack chain unfolds when a threat actor targets this specific developer using credential stuffing or targeted social engineering. The OSINT prerequisites enabling this vector are documented in `9-employee_notes.md` Finding 2, which explicitly maps the developer's username and identity to the company's internal Tidewater Risk Engine repository. Once the attacker gains unauthorized access to this developer's account, they can inject malicious code directly into the framework. The probable business impact is a severe supply chain compromise that allows persistent backdoor access to Helix's core backend APIs and causes massive financial miscalculations in maritime risk assessments.

## Vector 3: Vishing the Communications Manager
The entry point is the direct phone line of the Community & Communications Manager, Marleen Koster. The attack chain unfolds via a voice phishing (vishing) call where the attacker spoofs Helix IT support. Relying on the OSINT prerequisites from `9-employee_notes.md` Finding 3, which exposed her direct phone number, and `8-social_notes.md` Finding 2, which revealed the upcoming Rotterdam Maritime Week 2025 event, the attacker uses the event as a high-pressure pretext. They demand she urgently verify her mobile device synchronization for the upcoming press traffic, tricking her into approving a fraudulent MFA prompt. The probable business impact is initial access to a trusted communications account, enabling the attacker to send authenticated internal phishing emails to executives or manipulate external press releases to cause severe reputational damage.

## Vector 4: Physical Tailgating and Internal Network Implantation
This physical attack vector targets the corporate headquarters as the entry point, exploiting the predictable physical routine of the Head of Technical Operations, Sander de Boer. The scenario unfolds with a red team operative waiting at the building entrance at the exact time Sander finishes his run. The operative uses distraction and close proximity to tailgate the executive through the secure doors, subsequently plugging a rogue hardware implant into an unattended port on the internal network. This relies entirely on the OSINT prerequisites from `9-employee_notes.md` Finding 4, which tracked the executive's GPS fitness routines, and `6-registry_notes.md` Finding 1, which confirmed the exact registered headquarters address at Wilhelminakade 909. The probable business impact is catastrophic, as direct physical network access bypasses all external firewalls, granting the attacker immediate internal reconnaissance capabilities and a launchpad for ransomware deployment.
